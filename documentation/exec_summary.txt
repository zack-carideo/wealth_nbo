Executive summary
FCB's revenue growth depends on putting the right product in front of the right customer at the right time, and single-signal targeting leaves measurable lift on the table by ignoring journey context, timing, and economic value. The NBO framework combines four orthogonal signals — adoption likelihood, sequence position, timing, and expected value — into a unified composite score for every customer-product pair. The result is a single ranked recommendation per customer that is both likely to convert and commercially worth converting.
Propensity

Answers "how likely is each customer to adopt each product?"
Uses structured features: product ownership, balances, channel mix, behavioral aggregates
Produces a per-customer-per-product likelihood score
Filters out products the customer is structurally unlikely to take

Sequence

Answers "what is the natural next product in this customer's journey?"
Models historical patterns of product progression across the customer base
Captures journey logic that propensity-alone scoring misses
Surfaces sequentially relevant products even when their standalone propensity is moderate

Timing

Answers "when is each customer likely to act, not just whether?"
Uses survival/hazard methods on inter-purchase intervals
Converts static targeting into time-aware recommendation windows
Prevents wasted campaign spend on customers in dormant periods

Value

Answers "what is each product worth if this customer adopts it?"
Combines product margin with expected balance trajectory and tenure
Tilts the composite score toward commercially meaningful adoptions
Prevents over-recommending low-value products simply because they convert easily
